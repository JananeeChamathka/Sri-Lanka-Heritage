About Sri Lanka

The Democratic Socialist Republic of Sri Lanka is an island nation located at the southern tip of Asia. Known as the "Jewel of the Indian Ocean," Sri Lanka boasts a written history spanning over 3,000 years.

With vibrant traditions, ancient cities, stunning coastlines, hill country landscapes, and abundant wildlife, this island attracts millions of tourists every year from around the globe.

From the ancient rock fortress of Sigiriya to the sacred Temple of the Tooth in Kandy, from pristine beaches of Mirissa to the wild safaris of Yala — Sri Lanka offers an experience like no other.

Traditions & Culture

Discover the vibrant world of Sri Lankan traditions, customs and cultural heritage.

Sinhala & Tamil New Year

Celebrated in April, the Sinhala and Tamil New Year is Sri Lanka's most important cultural festival. Based on astrological calculations, people engage in cooking, transactions, and starting work at auspicious times.

  • Temple visits & offerings
  • Traditional poetry & songs
  • New Year games (Kabaddi, Ollinda Keliya)
  • Traditional sweets like Kavum & Kiribath

Vesak Festival

Commemorating the birth, enlightenment, and passing away of Gautama Buddha, Vesak is celebrated on the full moon day in May. The entire island is illuminated with colorful lanterns and decorations.

  • Vesak lantern displays
  • Dansal (free food distribution)
  • Meditation & observing sil
  • Temple pilgrimages

Esala Perahera (Kandy)

The Esala Perahera held at the Temple of the Tooth in Kandy is a world-renowned cultural pageant. Parading the sacred tooth relic, this grand procession spans 10 nights of spectacular performances.

  • Ornately decorated elephants
  • Traditional dancers & drummers
  • Whip crackers in traditional attire
  • Grand finale night of the Perahera

Sri Lankan Dance Forms

Sri Lanka has three main dance traditions: Udarata (Kandyan), Pahatharata (Low Country), and Sabaragamuwa dances. Kandyan dance is particularly famous worldwide for its elaborate costumes and rhythmic movements.

  • Kandyan dance - Ves dance
  • Devil dances (Pahatharata)
  • Sabaragamuwa dance tradition
  • Traditional drumming styles

Sri Lankan Painting & Art

Sigiriya frescoes, Kandyan era paintings, and ancient temple murals are integral parts of Sri Lankan artistic heritage. The traditional Kandyan painting style remains alive today in Buddhist temples across the island.

Hospitality & Greeting Customs

"Treating a guest as a god" is deeply rooted in Sri Lankan culture. Offering a cup of tea with biscuits to any visitor is a time-honored tradition. The respectful greeting "Ayubowan" wishes long life.

Special Tourist Places

Every place tells its own unique story — explore Sri Lanka's most remarkable destinations.

UNESCO Heritage

Sigiriya (Lion Rock)

Central Province

Sigiriya is a world-famous ancient rock fortress built by King Kashyapa I in the 5th century AD. Known as the '8th Wonder of the World', it features stunning frescoes, the Lion Gate, and a magnificent palace on top.

Ancient Fortress Fresco Paintings Hiking
UNESCO Heritage

Sri Dalada Maligawa (Temple of the Tooth)

Kandy

The Temple of the Tooth in Kandy houses the sacred left canine tooth relic of the Buddha. Venerated by Buddhists worldwide, it is famous for the annual Esala Perahera procession and its stunning Kandyan architecture.

Sacred Tooth Relic Buddhist
UNESCO Heritage

Galle Fort

Galle

Built by the Portuguese in the 16th century and later expanded by the Dutch, Galle Fort is a stunning fusion of European and South Asian architectural styles. Walk along ancient ramparts, explore museums, and enjoy ocean views.

Colonial Architecture Museums Boutique Shops
Wildlife

Yala National Park

Southern Province

Yala is Sri Lanka's most famous national park, home to leopards, elephants, sloth bears, and diverse bird species. Safari tours offer thrilling wildlife encounters in a stunning natural landscape.

Safari Leopards Elephants
Hill Country

Ella

Uva Province

Ella is a charming hill country town in Sri Lanka. The Nine Arch Bridge, Ella Waterfall, Little Adam's Peak, and endless tea plantations make it a must-visit destination for nature lovers and hikers.

Waterfall Tea Plantations Hiking
UNESCO Heritage

Anuradhapura Ancient City

North Central Province

Anuradhapura was Sri Lanka's first capital, flourishing from the 4th century BC to the 11th century AD. Highlights include Ruwanwelisaya, Jethavanaramaya, and the sacred Sri Maha Bodhi tree — the oldest historically documented tree in the world.

Ancient City Temples Stupas
UNESCO Heritage

Polonnaruwa Ancient City

North Central Province

Sri Lanka's second capital, Polonnaruwa reached its golden age under King Parakramabahu I. Famous for its stone sculptures, the Parakrama Samudra reservoir, and magnificent Buddhist shrines.

Stone Carvings Buddha Statues History

Pinnawala Elephant Orphanage

Sabaragamuwa Province

Founded in 1975, Pinnawala Elephant Orphanage cares for orphaned and injured elephants. The highlight for visitors is watching the baby elephants being bottle-fed and the herd bathing in the river.

Dambulla Cave Temple

Central Province

The Dambulla Cave Temple is the largest and best-preserved cave temple complex in Sri Lanka. It houses over 150 Buddha statues and exquisite ceiling frescoes spanning 2,000 years of history.

Sri Lankan Cuisine

Discover the island's rich, aromatic, and spicy culinary traditions.

Rice & Curry

The staple Sri Lankan meal. Steamed rice served with an array of spicy fish, meat, or vegetable curries with sambols and chutneys.

Hoppers (Appa)

Bowl-shaped pancakes made from fermented rice flour. Served plain, with egg (egg hopper), or with milk (milk hopper) for breakfast or dinner.

Kottu Roti

Chopped roti bread stir-fried with vegetables, egg, and/or meat. A popular street food dish often served with curry sauce.

Kavum (Oil Cakes)

A traditional sweet made from rice flour, coconut milk, and treacle, deep-fried until golden. Essential for the Sinhala New Year celebration.

Ceylon Tea

World-famous Ceylon tea is grown in Sri Lanka's central highlands. A steaming cup of tea is central to Sri Lankan hospitality and daily life.

Sambol

Spicy condiments served alongside meals. Coconut sambol (pol sambol), onion sambol, and chili sambol (lunu miris) are the most popular varieties.
